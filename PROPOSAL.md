# PROPOSAL: jaschablume.nl deep overhaul

## 1. Positioning statement

Jascha Blume is an artist who works in the gap between what is said and what is received -- the time-lag of translation, the space between presence and absence, the grey zone between the Deaf world and the hearing world. Film, photography, installation, social enterprise, and writing are not separate disciplines but different instruments for the same inquiry: what does it mean to be present but offset, inside and behind at the same time?

## 2. Site architecture

1. **Hero** -- Name, the core claim in Jascha's own voice, the sentence that places him. Not "sensory translation lab" (too clinical) but something from the DNA: the grey zone, the time-lag, the signo ergo sum.

2. **Position** -- A short text (3 paragraphs max) that explains the practice in first person. Not a bio. A statement of what the work is about and where it comes from. Draws on the EDIT-GLOBAL VILLAGE essay, the monologue, the Rietveld thesis.

3. **Timeline / Arc** -- A chronological spine showing the practice across time: 2000 (Gewoon Doof, IDFA), 2005 (Gallaudet), 2008-2013 (Rietveld), 2012 (I Sign I Live), 2013 (DovenExpo, Graduation), 2015 (Here & There, L'Altro Spazio, Van Abbemuseum), 2016 (Deconstructing Presence, ART CITY Bologna), 2026 (Crip Minds). This is the strongest structural addition -- it shows the arc, the seriousness, the duration.

4. **Selected Work** -- 6-8 projects with real depth. Not cards with tags. Each project gets: title, year, medium, one paragraph of substance, and the question it asked. Projects chosen for range AND coherence: I Sign I Live, Here & There, De Gebarentaaltolk en Ik, Kalokagathia, L'Altro Spazio, Deconstructing Presence / Van Abbemuseum, Crip Minds.

5. **Thinking** -- A section that surfaces the intellectual formation without being academic. The thesis (nominated, Rietveld 2013), the key frameworks (GIFs and sign language share structure, the tussenruimte, the time-lag as artistic engine). Pull quotes from the DNA. This is what the current site completely buries.

6. **Recognition** -- Awards, screenings, institutional connections in a clean list. Moscow, Seattle, Palermo, IDFA, Van Abbemuseum, Kunstinstituut Melly, Boerhaave Museum, Mister Motley, Lonely Planet Bologna. This is what gives a curator confidence.

7. **Contact / Collaborate** -- Studio Blume, Amsterdam & Bologna. Email. One sentence about collaboration.

## 3. Copy direction

**Voice:** First person, direct, specific. Jascha's own sentences where possible -- the DNA pack is full of lines that are already written and already perfect. The register is: artist who has been thinking about this for 20 years, not startup founder pitching accessibility.

**What to emphasize:**
- The time-lag as the engine of everything (this is the single most distinctive thing about the practice -- nobody else has this)
- The intellectual formation: Warhol, Baudrillard, Brecht's gestus, Tarkovsky, Benjamin -- this is an art practice with real intellectual depth
- The range: film, photography, installation, social enterprise, writing, VJ work, community organizing
- The coherent thread: every project asks about the gap between what is said and what is received, between presence and absence
- Gallaudet as a formative experience -- the place where the lag disappeared

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't reduce the practice to "accessibility design" -- that's one instrument, not the whole practice
- Don't use corporate language (cultural entrepreneur, inclusive business solutions)
- Don't cite disability studies scholarship -- Jascha is explicit that his formation is art, not academia
- Don't use "Sensory Translation Lab" -- it sounds like a UX consultancy, not an artist's practice

## 4. Design direction

**Typography:** Option C -- serif for headings, grotesque for body. Specifically: a strong serif (Playfair Display works, or something with more weight like DM Serif Display) for project names and section headings, and a clean grotesque (Inter or similar) for body text. IBM Plex Mono stays for labels/metadata. This reads as European design tradition, not tech.

**Color:** Moving away from the neon-green-on-black direction. That reads as tech/startup. Instead:
- Warm off-white background (#f5f2ed or similar -- paper, not screen)
- Deep near-black text (#1a1a1a)
- One accent: a warm amber/ochre (#c17f24 or similar) -- warm, European, not clinical
- Surface color for alternating sections: slightly warmer (#ece8e1)
- This palette says: publishing, gallery, editorial. Not portfolio template.

**Layout:**
- Generous whitespace, generous type sizes
- Timeline as a vertical spine with years as anchors -- the strongest structural element
- Projects as full-width blocks, not cards in a grid
- Pull quotes set large, in the serif, as section dividers
- The whole thing should breathe. It should feel like a well-designed exhibition catalogue, not a tech portfolio.

**Motion:** Minimal. No canvas animation. No particle effects. Scroll-reveal for sections (with noscript fallback). The work should carry it.

## 5. What to surface that the current site buries or omits

- **The thesis** -- nominated for best thesis prize, Rietveld 2013. This is important institutional evidence.
- **The intellectual formation** -- Warhol, Baudrillard, Brecht, the GIF-as-sign-language argument. The current site has zero of this.
- **The timeline/arc** -- 25+ years of practice. The current site makes it look like 3-4 projects.
- **Here & There** -- screened in Toronto, Hong Kong, Rome, New Delhi. International reach.
- **De Gebarentaaltolk en Ik** -- the interpreter installation. This is the most conceptually precise work in the archive and it is completely absent from the current site.
- **Deconstructing Presence** -- Boerhaave Museum, alongside Douwe Draaisma. Institutional legitimacy.
- **DovenExpo** -- 13 artists, 46 works. Community building as practice.
- **The ramp story** -- 11 years of fines. This is the single most powerful concrete detail about accessibility as lived reality.
- **Mister Motley** -- published author in a respected Dutch arts magazine. Writing as part of the practice.
- **Kunstinstituut Melly** -- workshop facilitation. Not just making work but facilitating others' encounters with it.
- **Crip Minds** -- the current project. A disability culture publication. Shows the practice is active and evolving.
- **Jascha's own words** -- the DNA has dozens of perfectly written lines that should be on the site verbatim.
