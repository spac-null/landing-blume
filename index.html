Signo ergo sum. I sign, therefore I exist.

I am an artist and I am Deaf.

I make films, photographs, installations, and spaces. I co-founded a bar in Bologna where 80% of the staff are people institutions call too complicated to hire. I write for Mister Motley. I built a disability culture publication. The medium changes. The question stays the same: what happens in the gap between what is said and what is received?

The practice

An interpreter does not translate simultaneously. They must first understand a few sentences, then choose. I always receive things a few seconds late. If someone tells a joke, I laugh after everyone else. I live in a slightly different time zone than whatever room I am in.

That gap -- being present but offset, inside and behind at the same time -- is the central problem of my work. I have also been in the room where the gap disappears entirely, where everyone shares a language and nobody needs to translate. That room exists. It just does not last. The grey zone between worlds is where I draw my energy. I stay there deliberately.

I am not a disability studies academic. My formation is through art, through making things, through living in a Deaf body in a hearing world. I do not want to be called "you-know-that-deaf-artist." I am the artist Jascha Blume. To bring something deep from within yourself to the surface -- that is something more individual than being Deaf. That is something individual, not collective.

Sometimes I want to move to that place that no longer exists. Where should I go? When you mix blue and yellow you get green. I wish I could get that green back into its two original colours. Probably not. The exact blue and yellow live only in your memory.
-- on the temporary Deaf community
Arc

Twenty-five years of practice

  1. 2000 Gewoon Doof -- documented at 13, attending two schools simultaneously. The film screens at IDFA, Amsterdam.
  2. 2005 Gallaudet University, Washington DC. The world's only Deaf university. No interpreters. No time-lag. Everything direct.
  3. 2008 -- 2013 Gerrit Rietveld Academie, Amsterdam. Audio Visual department. Thesis: Behind the GIF Images and Sign Language -- nominated for best thesis prize.
  4. 2010 -- 2012 Rietveld Uncut performance, Brakke Grond. Group exhibition OMG, de Oude Kerk, Amsterdam. Storage Space Exhibition #4.
  5. 2011 De Gebarentaaltolk en Ik -- three-channel video installation. A poem from a dream, translated live by two interpreters, all three running simultaneously. The first time I could watch my own language without the translation lag.
  6. 2012 Ik Gebaar, Ik Leef / I Sign, I Live -- feature documentary, co-directed with Anja Hiddinga (interviewer & co-director). Best Documentary awards in Moscow, Seattle, and Palermo. Screened in 20+ countries.
  7. 2013 DovenExpo, Amsterdam -- co-organized exhibition of 13 Deaf artists, 46 works. Mini solo exhibition, MuteSounds Festival, Scheveningen. Graduation show, Rietveld. De Gebarentaaltolk en Ik screened at Sadho Poetry Film Festival, New Delhi. Honourable Mention, Picture This Film Festival, Calgary.
  8. 2013 -- 2015 Van Abbemuseum, Eindhoven. Museum guide for the Special Guests accessibility programme. Monthly sign language tours through exhibitions including Ahmet Ogut's Exploded City.
  9. 2013 -- 2015 NDSM Wharf, Amsterdam (Kunststad, M10). Took over a full container and built it into a Deaf-led shared studio -- Deaf majority by design, hearing artists welcome as minority. The last Amsterdam base before Italy became the main pull.
  10. 2015 Here & There -- short documentary. Deaf twins in an Addis Ababa slum and Deaf sisters on a Groningen farm. Screened in Toronto, Hong Kong, Rome, New Delhi. Young Art Night prize, Van Abbemuseum.
  11. 2015 Co-founded L'Altro Spazio, Bologna -- cocktail bar, cultural space, social cooperative. 80% staff from vulnerable backgrounds. Braille menus. Staff trained in Italian Sign Language. Listed in Lonely Planet.
  12. 2016 Claim a Piece of Territory -- three exhibitions curated at L'Altro Spazio for ART CITY Bologna. Deconstructing Presence -- performance at Museum Boerhaave, Leiden, alongside professor Douwe Draaisma.
  13. 2026 Crip Minds -- disability culture publication, active and publishing. Workshop facilitator, Kunstinstituut Melly, Rotterdam.

Selected work

Projects across media

Film, photography, installation, social enterprise, community organizing, writing. Different instruments for the same inquiry.

I Sign, I Live

2012 Feature documentary, 58 min — co-directed with Anja Hiddinga

A film I co-directed and conducted interviews for with Anja Hiddinga. We visited Europe's only home for elderly Deaf people — they told us about their youth, when sign language was forbidden, about the struggles against embarrassment and indignity. All dialogue is in Dutch Sign Language. My role: interviewer and co-director. The split-screen editing — camera images running as commentary on what we encountered — was a shared formal decision that came out of the collaboration.

Best Documentary: International Disability Film Festival, Moscow. Best Documentary: Seattle Deaf Film Festival. Best Documentary: Sole Luna Festival, Palermo. Screened in Tokyo, Belgrade, New Delhi, and 20+ countries.

De Gebarentaaltolk en Ik

2011 Three-channel video installation

A poem from my dream, translated by an interpreter into spoken Dutch, translated back by a second interpreter into Dutch Sign Language. Three videos, three people, recorded separately, synchronised. When the videos run simultaneously, my focus shifts to the difference between me and the interpreter -- not the content but the gap. The tension in that difference is what I find interesting.

Screened at 4th Sadho Poetry Film Festival, New Delhi, 2013.

Here & There

2015 Short documentary, 10 min, silent

Seventeen-year-old Deaf twins Ekram and Euphrates in an Addis Ababa slum, and Deaf sisters Hilene and Willemijn on a Groningen farm. Their lives are totally different, yet the same. The Deaf community around the world is one big virtual country without borders. Dutch and Ethiopian Sign Languages with English subtitles. Cinematographer: Bart Koolen.

Screened in Toronto, Hong Kong, Rome, New Delhi.

L'Altro Spazio

2015 -- ongoing Social cooperative, Bologna

A cocktail bar and cultural space co-founded with Nunzia Vannuccini. Eighty percent of staff come from backgrounds that elsewhere make them too complicated to hire. Braille menus. All staff trained in Italian Sign Language. Dinners in the dark, guided by blind staff. Multiple locations across Bologna. Listed in Lonely Planet.

We applied for a wheelchair ramp permit in a heritage zone. We waited eleven years. We installed it anyway. Accumulated fines. The ramp is a complete picture of what accessibility means in practice: something obviously necessary, technically straightforward, and structurally blocked by institutions that were never designed with disabled people in the room. We named a beer after it.

Kalokagathia

2020 Video portrait series, multi-channel installation

Nine collaborators filmed in intimate video portraits exploring the ancient Greek ideal of beauty and goodness in harmony. The research behind it: a mathematical formula for ideal facial proportion (the Marquardt Phi Mask, based on the golden ratio) was applied to real faces. People preferred the original, unmodified faces 63.7% of the time. The mathematically perfected face was less attractive. The formula works. What it produces is horror. The corrected face is more perfect and less desired.

Deconstructing Presence

2016 Performance

Museum Boerhaave, Leiden -- part of Perspectives on Diversity: The Cultural Life of Absence. Performed alongside professor Douwe Draaisma and comedian-writer Vincent Bijlo. As a Deaf person in a hearing space, presence is always partial, always mediated. The performance asks what culture is built in and around absence.

Artifical

2013 Photography

Technology brings us to the world of illusion. Everything will be fake, and that is the source of inspiration, not a problem. The sky becomes artificial. It dances constantly. Everyone is looking for something while it could be nothing.

Crip Minds

2026 Publication

A disability culture publication. Disability culture has built an enormous body of expert knowledge. Almost none of it reaches the places where culture gets made and interpreted. Not because it is hidden. Because nobody is looking.

Thinking

The intellectual formation

My thesis at the Gerrit Rietveld Academie -- Behind the GIF Images and Sign Language, nominated for best thesis prize, 2013 -- argued that repetition in GIFs, in sign language, and in installation works changes the viewer's experience of time. GIFs and sign language share a structure: both are time-based, both loop, both lose something the moment they stop. That loss is the message.

The thinkers I actually work with: Warhol on repetition and the copy. Baudrillard on the simulacrum -- the copy without an original. Brecht on the gestus: a single gesture that contains a whole paradigm, which is what brought me to sign language as artistic medium. Tarkovsky on sculpted time. Benjamin on what mechanical reproduction does to aura. Hume: "Repetition changes nothing in the object repeated, but does change something in the mind that contemplates it."

  • The time-lag An interpreter must first understand a few sentences, then choose. I always receive things a few seconds late. This gap is the engine of the work.
  • The tussenruimte The space between the work and the viewer. When does a work affect that space? The interpreter installation, the GIF thesis, the binnenkamer -- all ask what happens in this gap.
  • Meta-time A parallel timeline running alongside reality, containing memory, anticipation, imagination. Every head contains its own meta-time, which is why communication is always a translation problem.
  • The temporary sacred community Gallaudet. The Deaflympics. DovenExpo. L'Altro Spazio on a Friday night. The village that dissolves. The green that cannot be unmixed back into blue and yellow.
The physicality of Jascha's language as well as the necessity he feels to create visual art to "see himself speak" struck me as a rich source for a great variety of works. His experimenting with GIFs -- which have striking similarities to the coding of signs -- suggests a sensitivity to what is actual in the world of the moving image.
-- Sander Blom, Master of Film, Nederlandse Filmacademie
